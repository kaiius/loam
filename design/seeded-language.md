# Seeded language — design track

The keeper's ruling (2026-10-02): tanglekin founders start with a somewhat
complex understanding of English — like a 5-year-old's — so they can teach
the next generations language. What happens generation to generation is left
to evolution.

## The experiment

This is creole genesis in reverse. Instead of children inventing language
from nothing (Nicaragua), children inherit a full language and it evolves
under non-human learners: different vocal tracts, different senses,
different social structure. The question is not "can they learn English" —
it is "what does English become after a hundred generations of tanglekins."
If it creolizes into something unrecognizable, the experiment is working.

The seed is a gift, not a target. There is no pressure to preserve English.
The keeper teaches; the creatures decide — the Thoth/Hermes framing, in the
mechanism.

## "A 5-year-old's English," operationalized

A real 5-year-old commands thousands of words and genuinely complex grammar
(recursion, tense, questions). We seed the vocabulary honestly and the
combinatorics simply — syntax must evolve, not be installed:

- **Vocabulary**: English words with age-of-acquisition ≤ 5 (Kuperman AoA
  norms — a principled, published list, not a hand-picked one). Roughly a
  thousand words. Each maps to the creature's referent space where a
  referent exists: concrete nouns → world referents (fruit, tree, water,
  predator kinds), verbs → action drives (eat, climb, come, go), social
  words → social contexts (groom, play, warn). Words with no creature
  referent (abstract, human-specific) still seed as *forms* — infants hear
  them; they ground or die by selection.
- **Comprehension**: utterance → referent distribution. The v0.21 hearer
  side already does this; founders start with the mapping loaded instead
  of empty.
- **Production**: referent → utterance via simple templates — one- and
  two-word forms first ("big fruit", "come here", "danger up"), the classic
  telegraphic stage, plus a small set of fixed social formulas. No seeded
  recursion, no tense system. If the lineage needs those, it evolves them.

## Teaching: the existing machinery, now with something to teach

v0.21 already built the transmission path — it just had nothing worth
transmitting:

- **Ground-truth utterance log**: infants hear utterances paired with the
  true context (not inferred statistics) — the teacher is honest by
  construction.
- **Infant critical-period boost**: the learning rate is high early, then
  closes. Founder speech is the training corpus.
- **Probationary buffer with density-adaptive novelty threshold**: new
  forms earn their way in; noise doesn't.

## Evolution owns everything after the seed

- **Transmission bottleneck**: infants cannot learn a thousand words —
  episodic/working memory is finite. What survives is what small brains can
  learn and what social life rewards. Simplification is expected; watch for
  re-complexification (the signature that the language is alive, not
  decaying).
- **Social selection**: lexiconDistance already feeds mate choice (v0.21) —
  dialect becomes a mating signal, which couples linguistic divergence to
  the species-gate (v0.30). Dialects between tribes (v0.38) may become
  mutually unintelligible — speciation in the lexicon tracking speciation
  in the genome.
- **Drift and invention**: the probationary buffer admits novel forms;
  copying errors are mutations. No mechanism distinguishes "corruption of
  English" from "birth of Tanglekin" — correctly, because there is no
  difference.

## Probes

- **Transmission fidelity**: what fraction of the seed vocabulary survives
  N generations, and which semantic fields survive (expect: food, danger,
  social; at risk: the abstract).
- **Complexity trajectory**: vocabulary size and combinatorial depth over
  generations — decay curve or U-shape (decay then re-complexification)?
- **Dialect divergence**: inter-tribe intelligibility over time; does it
  track genetic distance?

## Relationship to the queue

- Builds on: v0.21 lexicon (transmission machinery — already shipped).
- Feeds: v0.38 tribe divergence (dialect divergence), v0.40+ cognitive
  ascent (teaching is one of the three selection pressures that turn the
  deliberative knobs; a seed language gives it something to work with).
- New design track; scheduled with the ascent program, not before the
  current build stream clears.

## Honest limits

- The founders' English is wide but shallow: rich words, simple
  combinatorics. A real 5-year-old's syntax is not seeded and cannot be —
  it must be grown.
- Founder production is template-based, not generative. The first
  genuinely novel utterance from a descendant is a milestone worth naming
  in the chronicle.
- Seeding biases the experiment toward human-like semantic fields. That is
  deliberate — it is the civilization bootstrap — but it means the null
  hypothesis ("they'd have invented something else entirely") is
  untestable in this world. A future world can run the unseeded control.
